The product

ARES

Aerospace Residue Elimination Service

A sensor-controlled inlet system that charges airborne dust and directs it away from the engine core. A built-in dust-exposure monitor records contamination levels so maintenance teams can target inspections and cleaning.

The problem

Fine dust, real damage

Fine desert dust ingested during take-off and landing can erode engine components, block cooling passages and reduce efficiency.

Existing separation methods struggle with the finest particles, which speeds up engine wear and pushes up operating and maintenance costs.

0-20μm

The particle size range that slips past conventional separators. That is finer than a human hair, and small enough to travel deep into the engine.

Today's approach

Why current answers fall short

Operators manage dust with a mix of avoidance and repair. None of these stop fine particles from reaching the engine.

Dust forecasting

Helps plan around dust events, but cannot remove dust that is already in the air.

Flight scheduling

Moving flights reduces some exposure, but operational avoidance can never eliminate it.

Protective coatings

Slow down erosion, but do not stop particles entering the engine.

Washing & repair

Deals with the damage once symptoms appear, after the dust has already done its work.

Conventional separators

Work on larger particles, but fine dust in the 0-20μm range can evade them.

How ARES works

Charge. Deflect. Collect. Monitor.

ARES tackles dust at the intake, before it reaches the parts that matter.

  1. 01

    Charge

    Sensors control the inlet system, which gives incoming dust particles an electric charge.

  2. 02

    Deflect

    Charged particles are directed away from the engine core, including the fine dust other systems miss.

  3. 03

    Collect

    Dust gathers in washable catch zones that are cleaned automatically after landing.

  4. 04

    Monitor

    A dust-exposure monitor records contamination levels to guide inspections and cleaning.

Why ARES

What makes it different

Monitoring meets separation

ARES combines dust monitoring with fine-particle separation. There is currently no airliner-focused solution that charges dust at the intake and uses washable catch zones cleaned automatically after landing.

Hard to copy

Our advantage lies in a validated combination of particle separation, accessible dust collection and exposure-based maintenance. Protectable IP may come from a novel collection mechanism or from how the system is integrated.
