The product

ARES

Aerospace Residue Elimination Service

A ground-mode electrostatic dust-capture cassette fitted in the core inlet of an airliner turbofan, paired with a dust-exposure monitor and a post-landing wash. By treating the core stream at taxi and idle across 5mm-pitch micro-gap channels — and stowing in flight — ARES cuts predicted core dust per flight by ~69%.

01The problem

Fine dust follows the streamlines

A particle's relaxation time scales with diameter squared (τ_p ∝ d²), so a 1μm grain is ~100× more streamline-following than a 10μm grain (9μs vs 777μs).

At take-off power, the fan's own centrifugal swirl removes most grains above ~10μm, but almost nothing below 5μm — and at taxi/idle swirl is ~5× weaker still (only 2.8% removal at 5μm). In our dusty-day budget (0.3mg/m³), 20 minutes of taxi/idle puts ~9.8g of dust into each engine core against ~3.1g during take-off and landing roll.

02Today's approach

Why current answers fall short

Operators manage dust with a mix of avoidance, coatings and repair — and naive full-inlet electrostatics cannot bridge a 1.4m nacelle radius at airliner flow speeds.

Naive full-inlet electrostatics

Fails by ~650×: steering a 5μm grain to the wall of a 1.4m inlet in milliseconds needs ~93m/s of sideways drift, whereas a 300kV/m bulk field delivers ~0.14m/s.

Fan swirl & inertial splitters

Centrifuge out coarse grains (>10μm) at take-off power, but remove only 0.1–2.8% of 1–5μm dust at ground idle.

Dust forecasting & scheduling

Helps plan around severe dust events, but cannot avoid background dust or jet-wash re-suspension during taxi.

Protective coatings

Slow blade erosion, but do not stop fine silicate dust from entering cooling passages and glazing hot-section surfaces.

Reactive washing & repair

Cleans or replaces hardware on fixed schedules or after EGT (Exhaust gas temperature margin: how much hotter an engine can run before reaching its limit. Dust damage eats into it.) margin degrades, without per-flight exposure data.

03How ARES works

Shrink the distance, not just boost the field

Instead of steering dust across the whole nacelle, ARES places a 36-channel, 5mm-pitch micro-gap cassette in the core inlet (10–15% of mass flow, 0.58m² annulus) where grains only need to drift 2.5mm to a collector wall.

  1. 01

    Corona charge

    25μm corona (Corona discharge: a faint, controlled electrical glow around a thin high-voltage wire. It fills the air with ions, which stick to dust grains and charge them.) wires on each channel centreline (2.5kV negative DC, j = 10mA/m²) generate N ≈ 3×10¹⁴ ions/m³, charging grains with a 3.5ms time constant during the 7.3ms idle residence time.

  2. 02

    Micro-gap capture

    A 1MV/m electric field drives charged grains across the s = 2.5mm half-gap onto grounded 50μm titanium foil walls (92m² total collector area; 1.59m/s achievable vs 0.34m/s needed at 5μm).

  3. 03

    Stow & post-landing wash

    Active during taxi-out and taxi-in, stowed before take-off power (avoiding a 21.7kPa high-speed pressure loss), and rinsed after shutdown (~30L wash to existing sumps) to prevent back-corona (Back-corona: unwanted sparking inside the dust layer on the collector plates, which lowers capture. Regular washing prevents it.).

  4. 04

    Exposure index (DEI)

    An optical-scattering inlet probe (0.01–10mg/m³, 1Hz) and N1 (N1: the fan's rotation speed, which every airliner engine already records.) fan-speed logs integrate a per-flight Dust Exposure Index weighted by (N1/N1_ref)^2.5.

01 · Particle response

τp = ρp d² Cc / 18μ

Relaxation time grows with d². 9μs at 1μm against 777μs at 10μm: fine grains follow the streamlines, which is why the fan's centrifuge misses them.

02 · Charging

q(t) = qs · t / (t + τc),  τc = 4ε0 / (N e Z)

Field (Pauthenier) plus diffusion (White) charging. At j = 10mA/m², N ≈ 3.0×10¹⁴ m⁻³ and τc = 3.5ms against a 7.3ms residence: about two-thirds of saturation charge at exit.

03 · Migration

w = q E Cc / 3πμd,  qs = 4πε0 E a² · 3εr/(εr+2)

Stokes drag against the Coulomb force. In a micro-gap the same physics gives 1.59m/s at E = V/s = 1MV/m from only 2.5kV.

04 · Collection

η(d) = 1 − exp[ −(1/s) ∫0tres w(t) dt ]

Deutsch–Anderson for a turbulent, well-mixed parallel-plate channel, with w rising as charge builds. A ×0.5 exponent gives the pessimistic case.

05 · Pressure loss

Δp = (f L/Dh + K) · ½ρV²,  f = 0.316 Re−0.25

Dh = 2 × pitch, K = 1.2. 3.6kPa at 55m/s idle, but 21.7kPa (15% of core-inlet pressure) at 124m/s take-off: the reason ARES stows above idle.

06 · Power

P = V · j · Aw,  Aw = (2L / pitch) · Acore

Ion current, not dust charge, sets the power: almost every ion lands on a plate. 92m² of collector at 2.5kV and 10mA/m² gives 2.31kW.

Nominal design point: ~2.3kW electrical power, ~35kg per engine, and ~3.6kPa (3.6%) core-inlet pressure loss at idle (~1.2kPa target with streamlined entry/exit fairings, K ≈ 0.3).

Key assumptions

  • Reference engineGeneric widebody high-bypass turbofan (GEnx / Trent 1000 class), 2.8m fan, BPR 6 at idle
  • Air45°C, 101kPa: ρ = 1.107kg/m³, μ = 1.93×10⁻⁵Pa·s
  • DustQuartz-like, 2650kg/m³, εr = 4; mass-lognormal, MMD 8μm, σg = 2.0
  • Concentration0.3mg/m³ dusty day, ×2 at power from jet-wash re-suspension
  • Ground phases1,200s taxi/idle and 90s take-off and landing power per flight
  • Ion mobility2.1×10⁻⁴ m²/V·s (negative air ions)
  • LossesEntry and exit K = 1.2 (≈0.3 with fairings)

Known limits of the model

  • Deutsch–Anderson assumes well-mixed flow; fan swirl and particle bounce are not captured.
  • Mean field V/s is used; the local field near the wire is higher, so breakdown margin must be tested, especially at 80–90% humidity.
  • The fan-separation baseline is a simple centrifuge model and needs calibrating with CFD.
  • Back-corona depends on the resistivity of the dust layer, which varies widely for desert dust.
  • Every value is a screening estimate to be replaced with engine-manufacturer and site data.

06Why ARES

What makes it different

Ground-mode core-stream architecture

ARES pairs a stowable, 5mm-pitch micro-gap cassette sized specifically for the turbofan core stream at taxi and idle with an integrated post-landing wash and an optical Dust Exposure Index (DEI) monitor that can also ship standalone.

Four elements working together

Our candidate differentiators centre on four elements: (1) ground-mode operation with an in-flight stowed cassette, (2) a micro-gap multi-channel core cassette, (3) DEI exposure-based maintenance coupling, and (4) post-landing wash integration.

07Validation path

From model to engine

Every number on this page comes from first-principles modelling. This is how we plan to prove them, stage by stage.

  1. Stage 0

    Simulate

    CFD (Computational fluid dynamics: computer simulation of how air, and the dust in it, flows.) with particle tracking of the core inlet and cassette, plus electrostatic modelling of one cell, to refine the screening model.

  2. Stage 1

    Bench cell

    One 5mm channel, 0.4m long, in a 55m/s dust wind tunnel. Target: ≥60% capture at 2μm, no spark-over at 80% humidity.

  3. Stage 2

    Annular sector

    A 1/12 sector of the ring with its stow actuator and wash. Target: washing restores ≥95% of clean efficiency.

  4. Stage 3

    Full-scale ring

    Ground rig or engine test cell at idle, with an engine manufacturer. Targets: ≤1.5% pressure loss, ≤2.5kW, ≤35kg.

  5. Stage 4

    In service

    A trial with an airline on one engine type, starting with the dust-exposure monitor, which can ship on its own.

08Open challenges

What we still have to solve

Good engineering means being clear about the hard parts. These are the biggest open questions we are working on.

Stow mechanism

A moving part in the core inlet must fail safe (stowed) and never create debris. We are comparing a translating ring with bypass doors.

Pressure loss at idle

~3.6% of core-inlet pressure in the current model. Streamlined entry and exit fairings could bring this to ~1.2kPa.

Humidity, rain & ice

Humid, dusty air reduces the margin before spark-over, and water can short the plates. The supply is current-limited and shuts off when wet.

Ozone

Corona discharge makes a little ozone. We need to measure it and make sure none reaches cabin air.

Certification

Any engine modification needs the airworthiness authority and the engine manufacturer involved early, which is why we are seeking industry partners.

  • 04Electrical power: ~2.3kW per engine at j = 10mA/m² (2.5kV)
  • 05Installed mass: ~35kg per engine (92m² of 50μm titanium collector foil)

  • 07Post-landing wash: ~30L rinse restoring ≥95% clean efficiency
