The product

ARES

Aerospace Residue Elimination Service

A ground-mode electrostatic dust-capture cassette fitted in the core inlet of an airliner turbofan, paired with a dust-exposure monitor and a post-landing wash. By treating the core stream at taxi and idle across 5mm-pitch micro-gap channels — and stowing in flight — ARES cuts predicted core dust per flight by ~69%.

How it works Physics & sizing
Core-inlet cassette 36 concentric titanium-foil channels (L = 0.40m, 5mm pitch) behind the fan splitter.

The problem

Fine dust follows the streamlines

A particle's relaxation time scales with diameter squared (τ_p ∝ d²), so a 1μm grain is ~100× more streamline-following than a 10μm grain (9μs vs 777μs).

At take-off power, the fan's own centrifugal swirl removes most grains above ~10μm, but almost nothing below 5μm — and at taxi/idle swirl is ~5× weaker still (only 2.8% removal at 5μm). In our dusty-day budget (0.3mg/m³), 20 minutes of taxi/idle puts ~9.8g of dust into each engine core against ~3.1g during take-off and landing roll.

9.8g vs 3.1g

Core dust ingested per flight during taxi/idle (1,200s) versus take-off and landing power (90s) on a 0.3mg/m³ dusty day. Ground idle is where the fine-dust dose enters the core.

Today's approach

Why current answers fall short

Operators manage dust with a mix of avoidance, coatings and repair — and naive full-inlet electrostatics cannot bridge a 1.4m nacelle radius at airliner flow speeds.

Naive full-inlet electrostatics

Fails by ~650×: steering a 5μm grain to the wall of a 1.4m inlet in milliseconds needs ~93m/s of sideways drift, whereas a 300kV/m bulk field delivers ~0.14m/s.

Fan swirl & inertial splitters

Centrifuge out coarse grains (>10μm) at take-off power, but remove only 0.1–2.8% of 1–5μm dust at ground idle.

Dust forecasting & scheduling

Helps plan around severe dust events, but cannot avoid background dust or jet-wash re-suspension during taxi.

Protective coatings

Slow blade erosion, but do not stop fine silicate dust from entering cooling passages and glazing hot-section surfaces.

Reactive washing & repair

Cleans or replaces hardware on fixed schedules or after EGT margin degrades, without per-flight exposure data.

How ARES works

Shrink the distance, not just boost the field

Instead of steering dust across the whole nacelle, ARES places a 36-channel, 5mm-pitch micro-gap cassette in the core inlet (10–15% of mass flow, 0.58m² annulus) where grains only need to drift 2.5mm to a collector wall.

  1. 01

    Corona charge

    25μm corona wires on each channel centreline (2.5kV negative DC, j = 10mA/m²) generate N ≈ 3×10¹⁴ ions/m³, charging grains with a 3.5ms time constant during the 7.3ms idle residence time.

  2. 02

    Micro-gap capture

    A 1MV/m electric field drives charged grains across the s = 2.5mm half-gap onto grounded 50μm titanium foil walls (92m² total collector area; 1.59m/s achievable vs 0.34m/s needed at 5μm).

  3. 03

    Stow & post-landing wash

    Active during taxi-out and taxi-in, stowed before take-off power (avoiding a 21.7kPa high-speed pressure loss), and rinsed after shutdown (~30L wash to existing sumps) to prevent back-corona.

  4. 04

    Exposure index (DEI)

    An optical-scattering inlet probe (0.01–10mg/m³, 1Hz) and N1 fan-speed logs integrate a per-flight Dust Exposure Index weighted by (N1/N1_ref)^2.5.

Architecture & mode logic Core-inlet cassette behind the fan splitter · ARES ON during taxi-out/taxi-in (idle) · Stowed / HV off during take-off, climb, cruise & landing roll · Post-shutdown wash.

Physics & sizing basis

First-principles screening model

Reference engine: generic widebody high-bypass turbofan (GEnx / Trent 1000 class, 2.8m fan tip, core inlet area 0.58m², V_idle = 55m/s). Concept-stage predictions prior to CFD and wind-tunnel validation.

Fractional capture efficiency at idle

Deutsch-Anderson parallel-plate model (L = 0.40m, pitch 5mm, s = 2.5mm, V = 2.5kV, E = 1MV/m, j = 10mA/m², t_res = 7.3ms) comparing fan swirl alone, nominal ARES, and a pessimistic ×0.5 derated case:

Per-flight core dust budget & sizing

Per engine on a 0.3mg/m³ dusty day (MMD 8μm, σ_g = 2.0; 1,200s taxi/idle + 90s take-off/landing power, cassette stowed at high power):

Phase Through engine Core (no ARES) Core (with ARES)
Taxi / idle (1,200s) 80g 9.8g 0.9g (2.4g derated)
Take-off / landing (90s) 50g 3.1g 3.1g (stowed)
Total per flight 130g 12.8g 3.9g (−69% nominal / −58% derated)

Nominal design point: ~2.3kW electrical power, ~35kg per engine, and ~3.6kPa (3.6%) core-inlet pressure loss at idle (~1.2kPa target with streamlined entry/exit fairings, K ≈ 0.3).

Why ARES

What makes it different

Ground-mode core-stream architecture

ARES pairs a stowable, 5mm-pitch micro-gap cassette sized specifically for the turbofan core stream at taxi and idle with an integrated post-landing wash and an optical Dust Exposure Index (DEI) monitor that can also ship standalone.

Engineering & validation roadmap

Our candidate differentiators centre on four elements: (1) ground-mode operation with an in-flight stowed cassette, (2) a micro-gap multi-channel core cassette, (3) DEI exposure-based maintenance coupling, and (4) post-landing wash integration — progressing from CFD and a single-channel 55m/s wind-tunnel cell to an annular sector rig and OEM ground-test cell.

Key metrics & targets

How we'll measure success

Screening design-point targets and the validation metrics we will track from bench cell to engine test bed.

  • 01Fine-particle capture: 62% at 2μm, 89% at 5μm (nominal idle)
  • 02Core dust per flight: reduced ~69% (12.8g to 3.9g on a 0.3mg/m³ day)
  • 03Idle pressure loss: ~3.6kPa (~1.2kPa target with streamlined fairings)
  • 04Electrical power: ~2.3kW per engine at j = 10mA/m² (2.5kV)
  • 05Installed mass: ~35kg per engine (92m² of 50μm titanium collector foil)
  • 06Fail-safe stow & arc/wet inhibit before take-off power
  • 07Post-landing wash: ~30L rinse restoring ≥95% clean efficiency
  • 08DEI correlation with borescope findings and engine wear
