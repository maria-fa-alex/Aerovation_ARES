Aerovation presents ARES

Less contamination — more elevation.

ARES is a ground-mode electrostatic dust-capture cassette in the turbofan core inlet, paired with a dust-exposure monitor and post-landing wash to cut fine-dust ingestion during taxi and idle.

Discover ARES Meet the team

The problem

Desert dust is wearing engines down

During ground operations, aircraft engines ingest fine desert dust that erodes components, blocks cooling passages and cuts efficiency. At taxi and idle, the fan's own swirl separates almost nothing below 5μm.

0–20μm

Fine particles follow the airflow streamlines; a 1μm grain is ~100× harder to centrifuge out than a 10μm grain.

~77% at idle

On a dusty day, taxi and idle put ~9.8g of dust per flight into each engine core versus ~3.1g during take-off and landing roll.

−69% core dust

Predicted core-dust reduction per flight in our nominal screening model (from 12.8g down to 3.9g per engine).

How ARES works

Charge. Capture. Stow & Wash. Monitor.

By treating the core stream at taxi and idle inside a 5mm-pitch micro-gap cassette, ARES shrinks the collection distance to 2.5mm so electrostatic forces win.

  1. 01

    Charge

    During taxi and idle, 25μm corona wires at 2.5kV charge dust entering the 36 concentric channels of the core inlet.

  2. 02

    Capture

    A 1MV/m field across each 5mm channel drives charged grains just 2.5mm onto grounded 50μm titanium collector foils.

  3. 03

    Stow & wash

    The cassette stows out of the core stream before take-off power to avoid high-speed pressure loss, then washes after shutdown.

  4. 04

    Monitor

    An optical inlet probe and fan-speed logs compute a Dust Exposure Index (DEI) each flight to guide borescope checks and washes.
