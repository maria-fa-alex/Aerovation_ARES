The problem

Desert dust is wearing engines down

During ground operations, aircraft engines ingest fine desert dust that erodes components, blocks cooling passages and cuts efficiency. At taxi and idle, the fan's own swirl separates almost nothing below 5μm.

Swipe

0–20μmthe dust size that slips past the fan

Fine particles follow the airflow streamlines; a 1μm grain is ~100× harder to centrifuge out than a 10μm grain.

~77%of core dust enters during taxi & idle

On a dusty day, taxi and idle put ~9.8g of dust per flight into each engine core versus ~3.1g during take-off and landing roll.

−69%core dust per flight with ARES

Predicted core-dust reduction per flight in our nominal screening model (from 12.8g down to 3.9g per engine).

Figures are predictions from our first-principles concept model (dusty day, 0.3mg/m³). They have not yet been validated by testing.

How ARES works

Catch the dust while the aircraft is on the ground

Most fine dust enters an engine during taxi, when air moves slowly enough for electricity to pull it out. ARES does exactly that, then gets out of the way for take-off.

  1. 01

    Charge

    Thin high-voltage wires give passing dust grains an electric charge.

  2. 02

    Capture

    Charged grains are pulled 2.5mm onto metal plates, before they reach the engine core.

  3. 03

    Stow & wash

    The unit folds away before take-off and is rinsed clean after landing.

  4. 04

    Monitor

    A dust sensor records each engine's exposure, so airlines inspect and wash engines when they really need it.

Where we are

Concept proven on paper. Next: the wind tunnel.

Our first-principles model shows ARES can work at taxi and idle. We are now refining it with simulation, then building a single test channel.

Latest9 November 2026Concept design v1 complete: first-principles physics and sizing model.

  1. Stage 0

    Simulate

    Particle-tracking CFD (Computational fluid dynamics: computer simulation of how air, and the dust in it, flows.) and electrostatic modelling of one cell.

    Bench cell

    One 5mm channel in a 55m/s dust wind tunnel.

    Airline trial, starting with the dust monitor.
